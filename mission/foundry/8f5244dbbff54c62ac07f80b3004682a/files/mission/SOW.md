# Studio Drills — Build Plan & Statement of Work

Prepared for Paul · Draft for discussion

## What you're building

A practice companion for Logic Pro. You open it, it hands you the next drill: a goal, a few
constraints, a key, a chord progression and a tempo, and a short ordered list of moves. Each
move is written in plain production terms (any DAW), with a line saying which Logic tool to use. Drills never
hand you pre-written parts. You do the drill in Logic. If you want help on a move, a hint is one click away, with the right page of
Apple's guide, and the app notes that you used it. When you finish, you record how each move went. The
app keeps a running record of what was new to you, what you managed on your own and what to come back to.

Drills get harder in levels, and later drills combine things you learned earlier. That is where the
app finds out whether a technique actually stuck.

Later, if it works: a calibration step, genre-specific levels, smarter progression, timed re-quizzing,
starter project templates, feedback on what you made, and guidance for other DAWs.

## How the phases work

Small, Medium and Large describe relative effort, not dates. Small is days, Medium is one to two weeks,
Large is longer or depends on things outside the build. We lock timing and cost right before each phase.

## Phase 0 — Check the first drills in Logic *(Small, mostly your time)*

Runs alongside the start of Phase 1: the Level 1 drills are drafted first, and you check them while the
rest of the app is built.

Delivers: the four Level 1 drills as plain text, drafted from Apple's guide.
Unlocks: confidence that the drills are doable, named correctly in your Logic version and pitched at
your level.
Needs from you: an evening in Logic 12.4 working through them.

## Phase 1 — The practice companion *(Small to Medium)*

Delivers: the five screens (today's drill, the drill itself, finishing a drill, your path through the
levels, and a page per technique); twelve genre-neutral drills across three levels; the "revisit" list; export
and import of your practice log, which is kept and never wiped; a demo version online.
Unlocks: two weeks of real practice, with a record you can read afterwards.
Needs from you: practice runs on your Mac. Your log is kept; export and import carry it across rebuilds.

## Phase 2 — Read the two weeks and decide *(Small, mostly your time)*

Delivers: a short readout of your practice log against what we said would count as working, not working,
or unclear.
Unlocks: the choice between writing more levels, changing how drills are pitched, or stopping.

## Phase 3 — Grow what worked *(Medium each, only if Phase 2 supports it)*

Options: a quick calibration step when you first start; genre-specific levels; progression that adapts to
your results; timed re-quizzing; using your kept practice log for labelling and evaluation.

## Phase 4 — Bigger bets *(Large)*

Options: starter project templates (on hold; needs a check on whether Apple's loops and instruments can be
shared inside them); tool-specific guidance for other DAWs, reusing the same drills; feedback on what you
made; automatic grading; keeping your practice history online across devices.
